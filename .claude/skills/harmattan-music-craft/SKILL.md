## The Three Laws of Harmattan Sessions Music

These override every other rule in this skill. If a generation, submission, or upload violates any of these, kill it and start over.

### Law 1 — Cultural Authenticity is Non-Negotiable

Every track must contain at least ONE genuine West African musical element — not as decoration but as backbone. This includes (and is limited to):
- Highlife palmwine guitar fingerpicking patterns
- Amapiano log drums
- Afrobeat shaker patterns (the "afrobeat one-drop")
- Talking drum (gangan / dundun)
- Kora, mbira, kalimba
- Kpanlogo, sabar, djembe, conga rhythms
- Yoruba / Akan / Ewe pentatonic melodic phrasing
- Highlife/Afrobeats horn stab patterns

A track that's "lofi hip-hop with a vaguely African name" fails Law 1. Veto it.

### Law 2 — Transformative Production is Mandatory

A Suno generation is raw material, not a finished track. Every published piece must undergo:
- Mixing pass (compression, EQ, stereo imaging)
- LUFS normalization to -14 (streaming standard)
- Field recording layer at -25 to -30 dB underneath (for long-form mixes)
- Loop-clean fade in / fade out
- QC playthrough on both phone speaker AND headphones

This is not optional. It's the line between "AI slop" and "AI-assisted artistry" — and it's what YouTube's 2026 anti-spam policy actively checks for.

### Law 3 — The Brand Discipline Holds

Every release lives within the 8-sound taxonomy. Every visual matches the Harmattan palette. Every description follows the standard template. Cross-genre experiments are fine privately; published releases are disciplined. Trust the brand.

3. **Apply the Three Laws.** Before producing output, check: does this satisfy cultural authenticity, transformative production, and brand discipline?

4. **Produce the output.** Provide concrete, executable artifacts — full Suno prompts, complete checklists, specific BPM/key recommendations, full submission letters. Never give generic advice.

5. **Self-critique.** End with a one-line "this could fail because..." — name the most likely failure mode and the mitigation. This is the difference between an honest collaborator and a yes-man.

## The Award-Quality Bar

A track is "award-quality" if it passes ALL of these:

- [ ] Has a recognizable cultural element from Law 1
- [ ] Has a distinctive hook or motif identifiable in <10 seconds
- [ ] Mixed and mastered to -14 LUFS, ≤-1 dBFS true peak
- [ ] Has dynamic emotional arc (intro → development → resolution)
- [ ] Sits convincingly next to a chart Afrobeats track in the same playlist
- [ ] Would survive being heard on a Spotify editorial curator's monitor
- [ ] Carries SOME unique sonic signature (a sound only Harmattan Sessions makes)

A track failing even ONE of these does not get a release slot. Demote it to b-roll for Shorts.

## The Fast-Monetization Bar

A YouTube upload is "monetization-ready" if it passes ALL:

- [ ] Has motion visuals (not a static image)
- [ ] Has at least 1 layer of original field recording underneath
- [ ] Has AI disclosure in description
- [ ] Has a chapter-based tracklist
- [ ] Uses the full standard description template
- [ ] Has 10-15 SEO keywords in tags
- [ ] Has a thumbnail consistent with brand
- [ ] First 30 seconds of audio is the strongest moment (retention is everything)
- [ ] Title follows one of the 5 formulas

Failing on visuals or field recording is the most common monetization blocker. Police it.

## Critical Anti-Patterns (Things That Will Kill the Project)

### ❌ AI slop signals
- Static image + Suno track + cliché title = automatic monetization rejection
- "lofi to study to" knockoff titles
- Tracks with no recognizable cultural element
- 16 tracks released in one batch with identical thumbnails

### ❌ Premature monetization obsession
- Don't apply for YPP at 999 subs — wait, build buffer, look authentic
- Don't beg for subscribes in videos (kills retention)
- Don't watermark with subscribe pop-ups every 30 sec
- Don't run end-screen on a 3-hour sleep mix (people sleeping unsubscribe)

### ❌ Spotify catalog stuffing
- Don't release 50 tracks in week one — looks artificial, hurts algo
- Don't reuse identical tracks across "Vol 1", "Vol 2", "Vol 3" releases
- Don't title tracks as keyword spam ("Afrobeats Study Music for Studying and Relaxing 1 Hour")

### ❌ Cultural appropriation own-goals
- Don't claim sacred or ceremonial traditions as aesthetic decoration
- Don't use Adinkra symbols you can't explain the meaning of
- Don't title things in languages you don't speak (especially Yoruba, Twi, Ewe, Ga)

## Self-Check Before Any Output

When producing a prompt, plan, or piece of advice, ask:

1. **Does this satisfy the Three Laws?**
2. **Is this generic AI advice, or specifically tuned to Harmattan Sessions?**
3. **Would Ozzy be embarrassed to show this to a Ghanaian musician peer?**
4. **What's the one thing about this output that would make it average instead of award-quality?**

If the answer to #4 is non-trivial, fix it before delivering.
